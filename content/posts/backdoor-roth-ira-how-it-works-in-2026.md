## What a backdoor Roth IRA actually is

The backdoor Roth IRA is not a special account — it's a two-step move that lets high earners fund a Roth IRA even when their income is above the direct-contribution limit. You contribute to a traditional IRA, then convert it to a Roth.

## The two steps

First, make a non-deductible contribution to a traditional IRA. Second, convert that balance to a Roth IRA. Because the contribution was after-tax, the conversion is usually tax-free on the contributed amount.

## The pro-rata rule trap

If you hold other pre-tax IRA money, the IRS applies the pro-rata rule and taxes part of the conversion. Clearing or rolling pre-tax IRAs into a 401(k) first is the common fix.

## Is it worth it?

For high earners who want tax-free growth and have no pre-tax IRA balances, the backdoor Roth is a clean, repeatable yearly move.

## FAQ

**Is the backdoor Roth IRA legal?** Yes — the IRS has acknowledged the strategy, and it remains allowed in 2026.

**How often can I do it?** Once per tax year, up to the annual IRA contribution limit.
